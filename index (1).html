Decoding Parameter & Sampling Analysis

How Temperature, Top-K and Top-P change the output of local LLMs (Llama 3.2 3B and Qwen 2.5 3B, run with Ollama)

2,160generations (0 errors)
30prompts
12settings (C01–C12)
2models, 3 runs each

Main findings

  • Temperature lowers consistency. Exact agreement of three runs falls from 53% to 35% (Llama) and from 65% to 43% (Qwen) between Temperature 0.2 and 0.7. From 0.7 to 1.0 the change is small.
  • No clear effect of Top-K or Top-P (20 vs 40, 0.8 vs 0.95) in this design: differences were about 2 points or less.
  • Accuracy was 100% for both models on the easy factual questions, so it could not separate the settings.
  • Hallucination depended on the model, not on the settings. Main rate on the three probe prompts: Llama 3.7%, Qwen 62.0%.
  • JSON: strictly valid in 100% of Llama outputs and 94.2% of Qwen outputs; most Qwen failures came from one prompt.

Time: median 0.34 s (Llama) and 0.32 s (Qwen) per call on a Colab T4 GPU, first call of each model excluded.

How the experiment worked

30 prompts (10 factual, 10 repeated, 10 structured JSON) were sent to each model under 12 combinations of Temperature (0.2, 0.7, 1.0), Top-K (20, 40) and Top-P (0.8, 0.95). Every prompt was run three times with seeds 42, 43 and 44. Because the seeds differ, the study measures consistency across seeds, not strict determinism.

Hallucination probes

Structured output (JSON)

Share of 36 runs per prompt (3 runs x 12 settings) that were strictly valid JSON, and that had the correct schema and values.

Neither model used markdown code fences, so lenient validity equals strict validity. S05: both models wrote the year as a string, but the prompt did not say “integer”. S07: Qwen often wrote an array of objects without braces. S06: Qwen often answered “green” for a lemon, while the reference is “Yellow”.

About and limitations

  • Small experiment: 30 prompts, three runs per prompt, two 3B models, one machine. Differences of a few percentage points are within chance.
  • Only two values each of Top-K and Top-P were tested, so no conclusion about them is possible.
  • Accuracy and hallucination labels were initially assigned using keyword rules; the project group members then reviewed the flagged answers.
  • The invented items in F08 and F09 were not found by a limited web search, which cannot prove that they do not exist.
  • 42 answers reached the 256-token limit and were cut off (mainly F09).

Full code, data, evaluation and the PDF report are in the project’s GitHub repository.
